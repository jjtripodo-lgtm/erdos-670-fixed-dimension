## The precise question

The maintained [Erdős Problem 670 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/670) and Ho's Remark 9 discuss a fixed dimension with an eventual coefficient-one diameter lower bound; the error rate may depend on that dimension. The accompanying theorem gives sets in one dimension, 458999, for every n≥68000 with separated unordered-edge distances and diameter below 0.74n². Choosing ε=1/4 contradicts that maintained fixed-dimensional formulation. It does not settle the plane, determine a least dimension, or optimize the coefficient.

Erdős's original Problem 20 appears on printed p. 6 of his 1997 chapter. An earlier source-access review visually inspected that page in a scanned reproduction, checked the volume's front matter, and read extracted chapter text. The original prose does not explicitly fix the quantifier order or uniformity in dimension. The present literature audit relied on that retained access receipt rather than freshly opening the page. The result here is stated against the maintained fixed-dimensional interpretation, without attributing a more precise assertion to the original page.

## Direct comparisons and classical ingredients

Ho's 2026 paper constructs examples along prime-power sizes with dimension growing with n. Its Theorem 1 and Remark 10 give asymptotic upper bounds; the sharper proved coefficient is approximately 0.885589, while the approximately 0.85411 value is a numerical exploration. Remark 9 explicitly leaves the fixed-dimensional question open. Ho reports a Lean formalization. The present claim is an explicit fixed-dimensional construction for every n beyond a threshold, not a first-formalization claim.

The modular ruler in this manuscript is an Erdős–Turán-type quadratic-residue construction. Erdős and Turán's 1941 Section I uses a quadratic residue sequence and proves the relevant uniqueness mechanism. The current ruler adds a cluster-dependent linear term and enlarged spacing for the geometry. This attribution identifies a classical ingredient; it does not establish how the author historically arrived at it or identify an earlier proof of this fixed-dimensional theorem.

Pach–Radoičić–Vondrák study many nearly equal distances, a different hypothesis. Brass and Baron permit repeated distance values under their respective formulations, unlike the distinct-unordered-edge rule here. The Euclidean distance matrix realization step is classical Schoenberg background. These comparisons have the access limits listed below.
