\title{Separated distances in fixed dimension}
\author{John Tripodo\thanks{ORCID: \href{https://orcid.org/0009-0006-9180-4953}{0009-0006-9180-4953}.}}

\begin{abstract}
A finite Euclidean point set is distance-separated if the lengths of any
two distinct unordered edges differ by at least one. Counting gives a
diameter lower bound of $\binom n2$, whereas Erd\H{o}s asked for a lower
bound with leading term $n^2$. We give two constructions in fixed dimension.
For every $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$, put $m_0=\lceil8192/\varepsilon\rceil$.
For every $n\ge4m_0$ there is a distance-separated $n$-point set in
$\R^{10m_0-1}$ with diameter $<(1/2+\varepsilon)n^2$.
A six-cluster construction gives arbitrarily large configurations in
$\R^{29}$ with diameter $<0.998n^2$, and an explicit prime-interval bound
gives diameter $<0.999n^2$ for every $n\ge400000$.
The constructions use two orthogonal circle families to control distances
within and between clusters separately. Their common geometric argument
reduces to a uniform operator estimate for the first result and three
rational positive-definiteness certificates for the second.
Lean~4 formalizations accompany the tolerance family and the
dimension-29 result for arbitrarily large sizes; the all-size corollary is a
written consequence.
\end{abstract}

\noindent{\small\textbf{Keywords:} separated distances, Euclidean diameter,
Gram completion, quadratic-residue rulers.}
